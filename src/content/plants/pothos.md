Pothos is widely grown as a forgiving houseplant because it tolerates a range of indoor conditions. Drooping can accompany dry soil, but it can also signal root trouble, so check the potting mix before reaching for the watering can.

## Watering without a schedule

The practical rule for pothos is to **let the potting mix partly dry**, then water thoroughly until excess water runs from the drainage holes.

Slightly soft or drooping leaves can indicate thirst, but the same symptom can appear when damaged roots cannot take up water. Feel the potting mix before responding: water if it is dry, and investigate drainage and roots if it is still wet.

Check more often in warm, bright conditions and less often when growth slows. The interval will vary with the pot, room and season.

## Light and variegation

Pothos is famous for tolerating low light, and that is true, but with a catch. **Variegation is light-dependent.** The cream, gold and white markings on Marble Queen, Golden and Snow Queen pothos are areas with less chlorophyll. In dim conditions the plant compensates by producing greener leaves that photosynthesise more efficiently.

- **Plain green types** (Jade, Neon): retain their color in lower light, though growth may slow
- **Variegated types** (Golden, Marble Queen, Manjula): need bright indirect light to hold their pattern
- **Highly variegated types** (Snow Queen): need the brightest indirect light you have

If your Marble Queen is coming in solid green, move it brighter and cut the reverted vine back to the last well-marked leaf.

## Making a pothos look full

A pothos can develop long, sparsely leafed vines. Pruning and adding rooted cuttings can help create a fuller-looking pot.

1. In spring, cut every long vine back by about a third, always just above a node.
2. New growth may develop from nodes below the cuts.
3. Root healthy cuttings in water or moist rooting mix, then plant established cuttings back into gaps in the same pot.

Results and timing depend on light, temperature, plant health and the number of cuttings used.

## Propagation

Pothos stem cuttings root readily when they include a healthy node.

- Cut a section of vine with **at least one node** and 2-4 leaves.
- Strip the leaf closest to the cut so the node sits bare.
- Stand it in a glass of water with the node submerged.
- Change the water when it becomes cloudy and wait for several healthy roots before potting into a well-drained mix.

## Common problems

**Yellow leaves.** Check the soil and drainage first, then compare the pattern with the [yellow-leaves guide](/problems/yellow-leaves-on-houseplants/). A single older leaf can yellow naturally, while several leaves plus persistently wet mix point toward root stress.

**Black stem bases and a sour smell.** Root rot. Unpot, cut away every mushy brown root, and repot into fresh dry mix in a smaller pot. Take healthy cuttings as insurance.

**Fine webbing under leaves.** This can indicate spider mites. Isolate the plant and use the [spider-mite guide](/problems/spider-mites/) to confirm the signs before treating it.

**Sticky residue on leaves or the shelf below.** Inspect closely for scale or mealybugs, isolate the plant if pests are present, and use a treatment labelled for the pest and plant.

## Pothos and pets

Pothos contains insoluble calcium oxalate crystals and is not pet-safe. Chewing it can cause oral irritation, drooling and vomiting. Keep the whole trailing plant out of reach and contact a veterinarian or animal poison service if a pet has chewed it and develops symptoms.

If you have a determined chewer, choose a [spider plant](/plant-care/spider-plant/) instead. It is non-toxic and grows in similar conditions.
