Most "low light houseplants" lists are optimistic. They include fiddle leaf figs and calatheas, which will slowly decline in a dim corner, alongside genuinely tolerant plants. The distinction matters if you are trying to keep something alive in a north-facing flat.

## First, what low light means

Low light is a spot where **you could not comfortably read a paperback at midday without a lamp**. That is typically several metres from a window, a north-facing room, or a corner away from the light source.

It does **not** mean a dark room. No plant photosynthesises in the dark, and a windowless space will kill even the toughest species eventually. For those rooms the answer is a cheap LED grow light on a timer, not a hardier plant.

It is also worth being clear that almost every plant on any such list *prefers* more light. "Low light tolerant" means it stays healthy and grows slowly, not that it is at its best.

## The genuinely tolerant twelve

Ranked roughly from most to least shade-tolerant.

### 1. ZZ plant (*Zamioculcas zamiifolia*)

The most shade-tolerant common houseplant. It stores water in underground rhizomes, grows well in offices lit mainly by ceiling panels, and forgives being forgotten for a month. Glossy, architectural and slow.

Water every three to six weeks; less in winter. Full details in the [ZZ plant care guide](/plant-care/zz-plant/).

### 2. Snake plant (*Dracaena trifasciata*)

Nearly as tolerant and even more forgiving of neglect. Upright sword-shaped leaves suit narrow spaces. The one risk is overwatering — in a dim corner the soil dries very slowly, so wait until it is bone dry throughout.

See the [snake plant care guide](/plant-care/snake-plant/).

### 3. Cast iron plant (*Aspidistra elatior*)

Named for its durability and genuinely deserving of it. A Victorian parlour staple precisely because it survived gaslit drawing rooms. Deep green strap leaves, extremely slow growing, and almost impossible to kill through neglect.

### 4. Pothos (*Epipremnum aureum*)

Trailing, fast and adaptable. The important caveat is variegation: golden and marble queen types lose their markings in dim light and revert to plain green. Choose **Jade pothos**, which is naturally solid green, if the spot is genuinely dark.

See the [pothos care guide](/plant-care/pothos/).

### 5. Heartleaf philodendron (*Philodendron hederaceum*)

Similar to pothos and slightly more shade-tolerant. Softer leaves, faster growth. Vines stretch in low light, so prune it back each spring to keep it full.

See the [heartleaf philodendron care guide](/plant-care/heartleaf-philodendron/).

### 6. Peace lily (*Spathiphyllum*)

One of few plants that will flower in medium-to-low light, though blooms are fewer the dimmer it gets. It also wilts dramatically when thirsty, which makes watering easy to judge.

See the [peace lily care guide](/plant-care/peace-lily/).

### 7. Spider plant (*Chlorophytum comosum*)

Tolerates medium and lower light, and is non-toxic to cats and dogs — a rare combination on this list. Produces fewer plantlets in dim conditions.

See the [spider plant care guide](/plant-care/spider-plant/).

### 8. Chinese evergreen (*Aglaonema*)

Patterned foliage in silver, cream and occasionally pink, which is unusual among shade-tolerant plants. The darker green cultivars tolerate less light than the pink and heavily variegated ones, which need medium light to hold colour.

### 9. Dracaena (*Dracaena fragrans*, *D. marginata*)

Upright and tree-like, useful for filling a corner. Sensitive to fluoride in tap water, so brown tips are common — use filtered water. Green forms tolerate lower light than variegated ones.

### 10. Parlour palm (*Chamaedorea elegans*)

The palm that actually works indoors. Soft fronds, compact size, genuinely shade-tolerant. Prone to spider mites in dry air, so keep humidity up and check the undersides of fronds.

### 11. Boston fern (*Nephrolepis exaltata*)

Handles low light well but demands humidity, which makes it a better fit for a dim bathroom than a dim living room. Needs consistently moist soil.

### 12. Lucky bamboo (*Dracaena sanderiana*)

Not bamboo at all. Grows in water or soil, tolerates low light easily. Sensitive to tap water chemicals — use filtered or distilled, and change it every couple of weeks if growing in water.

## What does not belong on these lists

Frequently recommended, and wrong:

- **Fiddle leaf fig** — a high-light plant. It declines steadily in dim rooms.
- **Monstera deliciosa** — survives medium light but will never fenestrate without brightness.
- **Calathea** — needs medium indirect light, not low. Its reputation for shade comes from "no direct sun", which is a different thing.
- **Succulents and cacti** — need direct sun. In low light they stretch badly within weeks.
- **Rubber plant** — tolerates medium light; in low light it drops its lower leaves.

## Caring for plants in dim spots

Two adjustments matter more than anything else:

**Water much less.** This is the big one. Less light means less water used, and dim corners are usually cooler with poorer air movement. Soil that dried in a week on a windowsill may take three weeks in a corner. Overwatering is how most low-light plants actually die — check the soil rather than following any schedule.

**Feed less.** A slow-growing plant cannot use much fertiliser, and the excess accumulates as salts. Half strength, every other month during spring and summer, nothing in winter.

Also worth doing:

- **Rotate the pot** a quarter turn every couple of weeks so all sides get some light
- **Wipe the leaves** monthly — dust meaningfully reduces the light reaching the leaf when there is little to spare
- **Clean the windows** and open blinds fully
- **Rotate plants** between a bright spot and the dim one every few weeks, if you have the option

## The grow light option

If your brightest spot is still not enough, a basic LED grow light on a timer for 10–12 hours a day genuinely solves the problem. They cost very little to buy or run, and they open up plants that would otherwise be impossible.

For a windowless room it is not an enhancement — it is the only thing that works.
