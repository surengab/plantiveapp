**For a small group of foliage houseplants, start with a white LED fixture that covers the leaves, an adjustable mount and a timer.** Follow the lamp's placement guidance and aim initially for about 12–14 total hours of light each day, including useful window light. Keep a regular dark period.

This is a starting setup for foliage plants such as [pothos](/plant-care/pothos/) and [heartleaf philodendron](/plant-care/heartleaf-philodendron/). Succulents, seedlings and plants being encouraged to flower may need different conditions.

## Check whether more light is the missing piece

Record where the plant sits, when direct sun reaches it, and whether curtains or nearby buildings block the window. Our [guide to measuring indoor light](/blog/how-to-measure-light-for-houseplants/) helps you compare positions.

Long gaps between new leaves and growth leaning toward a window can suggest insufficient light. Yellow leaves alone cannot diagnose it; check watering and pests as well. [University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/lighting-for-indoor-plants) describes both stretching under weak light and bleaching or scorching under excessive light.

Adding a lamp is particularly useful when a plant's location cannot change. If a brighter suitable window is available, try that before buying equipment.

## Choose a light that fits the space

Select the shape before comparing marketing claims:

| Your arrangement | A practical option | Check before buying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One plant on a side table | A directional white LED in a stable lamp | Can it illuminate the whole plant? |
| Several similar-height plants on a shelf | A linear LED fixture | Does the lit area cover the shelf ends? |
| A tall plant with a broad canopy | An adjustable overhead fixture | Can you maintain clearance as it grows? |

LEDs offer useful efficiency and relatively low heat output, although the fixture still needs ventilation. [Iowa State compares the available light sources](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/growing-indoor-plants-under-supplemental-lights/sources-supplemental-light-indoor-plants). Purple light is not a requirement; a suitable white light is easier to live with and makes leaf inspection simpler.

Wattage tells you electricity use. It does not, by itself, tell you what reaches a leaf. Look for output information measured at a stated distance and across a stated area, rather than relying on a large “equivalent wattage” printed on the box.

## Set distance by the fixture and the leaves

**There is no universal distance for every grow light.** A weak strip and a powerful panel can behave very differently at the same height.

Start with the manufacturer's recommendation for foliage plants. Measure to the upper leaves, not the soil. Keep the fixture adjustable, and place plants with similar needs and heights together. Check the edge of the lit area as well as its center.

[Iowa State's setup guide](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/growing-indoor-plants-under-supplemental-lights/how-set-supplemental-lights-indoor-plants) explains why intensity changes with fixture distance and why tall plants can shade shorter ones. A lamp that lights your table attractively may still leave most foliage outside its useful coverage.

Secure the light before watering. Keep electrical connections away from splashes and follow the fixture's clearance and installation instructions.

## Use a timer and allow darkness

Minnesota Extension recommends **12–14 total light hours for foliage houseplants**. A timer set from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m., for example, provides a 13-hour window in a dim room. Beside a useful window, you may need the lamp only to supplement part of that period.

Do not interpret overlapping daylight and lamp hours as extra clock hours: eight hours with both on is still eight hours of light exposure, at a higher intensity.

[Iowa State describes light quantity as a combination of intensity and duration](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/growing-indoor-plants-under-supplemental-lights/important-considerations-providing-supplemental-light-indoor-plants). Running a dim light around the clock is not a good substitute for a suitable fixture and a dark interval. [Christmas cactus](/plant-care/christmas-cactus/) and [poinsettia](/plant-care/poinsettia/) need special attention to uninterrupted nights when you want flowers or colored bracts.

## Adjust using new growth

Take a photo when you install the light. Record the lamp height and timer setting, then compare new growth over the following weeks.

- **Continued stretching:** check coverage, distance and whether the timer is actually switching on.
- **New bleaching or scorch near the lamp:** reduce intensity or increase distance, following the fixture guidance.
- **A pot drying faster:** check moisture more often; do not automatically pour in more water.
- **No quick visible change:** allow time for new growth before repeatedly moving the plant.

Change one setting at a time so you can tell what helped. Existing stretched stems will not shorten; use the [leggy-houseplant guide](/problems/leggy-stretched-houseplants/) for the next steps once growing conditions improve.
