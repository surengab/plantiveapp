**Scale insects can look like small shells stuck to houseplant stems or leaves.** If you find them, separate the plant from the rest of your collection, confirm the identification and start with physical removal for a small infestation. Sticky leaves are a clue, not a diagnosis.

## Identify the bumps before treating

Use good light and a magnifying lens. Look along the undersides of leaf veins, leaf stalks, young stems and protected leaf bases. [Maryland Extension's indoor-scale guide](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/scale-insects-indoor-plants) includes close photographs for comparison, including brown soft scale and fern scale.

Scale varies in shape and color. Some appears rounded and tan; other species have flatter, elongated covers. Aerial-root nubs, buds and old stem scars can also look raised. If the feature seems continuous with the plant's own tissue, stop before scraping it. A close photograph and advice from a local extension service can help resolve uncertainty.

### Soft scale versus armored scale

[UC IPM distinguishes two major groups](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/scales/): soft scale and armored scale. Both feed on plants, but their coverings and biology differ.

| Clue | Soft scale | Armored scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sticky honeydew | Often present | Not produced |
| Covering | Part of the insect's body surface | A separate protective cover |
| Young stage | Mobile crawlers | Mobile crawlers |

You do not need to pull apart every bump. The distinction matters because a treatment suitable for one group may not work for the other.

## Check the other causes of sticky leaves

Honeydew can fall onto lower leaves, the pot or a shelf. Black sooty mold may grow on the sugary residue. Cleaning the surface makes inspection easier, but does not remove insects hidden elsewhere.

The [RHS guide to indoor sap feeders](https://www.rhs.org.uk/biodiversity/indoor-plants-sap-feeding-insects) describes useful alternatives to check:

- **White cottony clusters:** compare with [mealybugs](/problems/mealybugs/).
- **Visible insects clustered around soft new growth:** look for aphids.
- **Small white insects that fly when disturbed:** consider whiteflies.
- **Fine webbing and pale stippling:** compare with [spider mites](/problems/spider-mites/).

Do not select a pesticide just because its packaging mentions “houseplant bugs.” Match the pest, the plant and the place of use.

## Remove a small scale infestation

1. **Separate the plant.** Leave space between its foliage and nearby plants. Inspect its former neighbors too.
2. **Work on one section at a time.** Gently remove visible scale with a suitable small tool, taking care not to gouge the stem or leaf. Stop if removal damages the plant.
3. **Wipe accessible leaves.** Use a damp cloth or paper towel, changing it as it becomes dirty. Check undersides as well as upper surfaces.
4. **Prune selectively.** If one removable stem is heavily infested, removing it may simplify control. Avoid stripping a plant of most of its healthy foliage.
5. **Inspect again regularly.** Include the leaf joints and concealed areas you could not reach during the first pass.

These physical approaches follow [University of Minnesota's indoor-pest guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/yard-and-garden-insects/insects-on-indoor-plants). The same source cautions against homemade soap sprays, which can damage plants. Use our [leaf-cleaning guide](/guides/clean-houseplant-leaves/) for routine care after pest control.

## When a product is needed

Horticultural oils and insecticidal soaps are among the options used against scale, but coverage, plant sensitivity and the insect's life stage affect results. Young crawlers are generally more vulnerable than protected settled stages. [UC IPM explains these treatment limits](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/scales/).

Choose a product whose label covers **scale, your plant and indoor use**. Follow its application rate, restrictions and repeat interval. Test on a small part of the plant where the directions call for it. Do not combine products or improvise a stronger mixture.

For a badly damaged plant covered in scale, disposal may be more practical than repeated treatment. [Maryland Extension recommends discarding heavily infested indoor plants](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/scale-insects-indoor-plants). Keep a discarded plant away from the rest of the collection.

## Tell whether control is working

Photograph a few marked inspection points before starting. At each follow-up, compare those areas and examine newly opened leaves. Record whether you see new settled insects, fresh honeydew or clean new growth.

Dead scale covers can stay attached. The [RHS recommends judging success by new growth remaining clear](https://www.rhs.org.uk/biodiversity/indoor-plants-sap-feeding-insects), rather than expecting every old bump to disappear. Keep checking even after the plant looks cleaner.

Inspect future purchases and plants returning from outdoors. The [bringing-plants-indoors checklist](/guides/bring-plants-indoors-for-winter/) helps catch hitchhiking pests before pots rejoin a shared shelf.
