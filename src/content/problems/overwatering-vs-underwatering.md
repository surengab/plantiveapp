An overwatered plant and an underwatered plant look remarkably similar: drooping leaves, yellowing, a generally unhappy posture. The treatments are opposite, so guessing wrong makes things considerably worse.

Five checks resolve it reliably.

## Check 1: the soil, two inches down

The most informative test, and it takes ten seconds. Push your index finger into the soil up to the second knuckle.

- **Damp or wet** → overwatering
- **Bone dry, possibly shrunk away from the pot edge** → underwatering

Do not judge by the surface. The top layer dries out within a day of watering while the root zone below stays saturated, which is exactly how people end up watering an already-drowning plant.

## Check 2: leaf texture

Squeeze a drooping leaf between your fingers.

| | Overwatered | Underwatered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feel | Soft, limp, sometimes squishy | Crisp, papery, brittle |
| Colour | Yellowing, often from the bottom up | Browning at edges and tips |
| Falling leaves | Drop while still green or yellow | Drop dry and crunchy |
| Edges | Soft, sometimes translucent | Crisp and curled |

## Check 3: the response to watering

An underwatered plant is dramatic and fast. Soak it and it stands back up within two to four hours.

An overwatered plant does not respond, or gets worse. If you watered a droopy plant and it still looks the same a day later, you almost certainly had the diagnosis backwards.

## Check 4: the pot and the smell

Overwatering leaves evidence around the pot:

- **Fungus gnats**: small black flies around the soil. They breed in consistently damp soil and are a reliable overwatering tell.
- **Mould or white fuzz** on the soil surface
- **A sour, swampy smell** from the pot
- **Green algae** on the soil or the sides of a terracotta pot
- **Water in the saucer** that never disappears

Underwatered pots feel noticeably light when lifted, and the soil pulls away from the sides leaving a visible gap.

## Check 5: the roots

The definitive test if the others are ambiguous. Tip the plant out and look.

- **Firm, white or pale tan roots** → the roots are fine, look elsewhere for the cause
- **Brown, soft, slimy roots that smell sour** → [root rot](/problems/root-rot/) from overwatering
- **Dry, brittle, shrivelled roots** → prolonged underwatering
- **Roots tightly circling the pot** → root-bound, which mimics underwatering because there is no soil left to hold water

## The third possibility: too little, too often

There is a middle failure mode that produces a permanently unhappy plant with no dramatic symptoms.

If you give a small splash of water every few days, only the top inch ever gets wet. The deeper roots, where most water uptake happens, stay dry, while the surface stays damp enough to invite fungus gnats and surface mould.

**The fix:** water less often but far more thoroughly. Every time you water, pour slowly until water runs freely from the drainage holes, then let it drain completely. Then leave it alone until that plant's soil is dry at the appropriate depth.

## When soil repels water

If soil has dried out completely it can become hydrophobic. Water beads on the surface, then channels down the gap between soil and pot and straight out the bottom, leaving the root ball dry.

**To rehydrate it:** stand the pot in a tray or sink with a few inches of water for 20-30 minutes. The soil wicks moisture up from below until the surface darkens. Then drain it fully.

A single drop of dish soap in the water breaks the surface tension and helps stubborn cases.

## When in doubt, wait

Most houseplants recover from underwatering within hours. Recovery from overwatering takes weeks or months, if it happens at all.

If you have run the checks and genuinely cannot tell, **wait two more days and check again**. That is the low-risk error.
