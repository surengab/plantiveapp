White mold on plant soil is usually a fungus feeding on organic matter in potting mix that stays damp, and it rarely harms the plant directly. It is a signal to let the surface dry and improve airflow, not a reason to throw the plant out, but first confirm that the white material really is mold.

## What is the white stuff on top of my plant soil?

Four things commonly show up as white on or in potting mix. Texture is the quickest way to tell them apart.

| What you see | Texture | Where | Most likely cause |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soft white fuzz or web-like threads | Fluffy, wipes away easily | Surface of damp mix | Fungal growth |
| White or yellowish crust | Hard, dry and crunchy | Soil surface, pot rim, outside of clay pots | Mineral or fertilizer salts |
| Cottony clumps | Waxy, with small insects underneath | Stem base, soil line, roots | Mealybugs |
| White granules | Hard, lightweight, uniform | Mixed through the soil | Perlite |

Perlite is part of the mix, not something growing on it, so it needs no treatment. If you lift a cottony clump and find a soft insect, stop here and follow the steps for [getting rid of mealybugs](/problems/mealybugs/).

## Fuzzy white mold: what it is and why it grows

Most fuzzy white growth on houseplant soil is fungal threads (mycelium) from fungi that live on dead organic matter. [UF/IFAS Extension](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/PP377) notes that these fungi feed on organic matter in the potting soil, that the mycelium is sometimes visible on the top layer, and that they are not necessarily harmful to plants. It links fungal growth in potting soil to high humidity, low light and warm temperatures.

Indoors, the usual pattern is a surface that rarely dries: frequent small waterings, a pot sitting in water, a dim spot or little air movement. Mixes rich in peat, bark or compost give the fungi plenty to feed on.

### Is it harmful to the plant?

The growth itself is usually cosmetic. One practical exception: UF/IFAS notes that some fungal mats become dense and water-repellent, which can stop water and nutrients from soaking in. If water beads on the surface or runs down the inside of the pot, break up the mat.

The bigger message is about moisture. A mix that stays wet for long periods is the same condition that favors root problems and gnats.

## How to get rid of white mold on soil

### 1. Scrape it off

Use a spoon to lift off the fuzz and the thin layer of mix it is growing on, ideally outdoors or over a bag. Loosen the surface lightly so it can dry, and top up with fresh potting mix if you removed much.

### 2. Let the surface dry as far as the plant tolerates

This depends on the species. Peace lilies and calatheas prefer evenly moist mix, so the goal for them is to avoid soggy mix, not to let them wilt. Plants like fiddle-leaf figs can have the top of the mix dry out between waterings. If you are not sure what your plant is, Plantive can identify it from a photo and show its watering and light guidance.

### 3. Fix drainage and airflow

Use a pot with drainage holes, and empty the saucer or decorative outer pot after watering so the plant does not sit in water. Move the plant to the brightest spot that suits the species and give it some space from its neighbors.

### 4. Water by the mix, not the calendar

Check the moisture below the surface before watering, not just the top. If you cannot tell whether the plant is too wet or too dry, work through [overwatering vs underwatering](/problems/overwatering-vs-underwatering/), and see [how to water houseplants](/blog/how-to-water-houseplants/) for technique.

## White crust on potting soil: salt buildup

A hard white crust is usually salt, not mold. [Oregon State University Extension](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/news/soluble-salts-damaging-houseplants) explains that fertilizer dissolved in water becomes a soluble salt, and the salts stay behind when the water evaporates. Minerals in hard tap water leave similar deposits on the mix and the pot.

Salt buildup can cause brown leaf tips, wilting, lower leaf drop and little new growth, so compare the plant with the causes of [brown leaf tips](/problems/brown-leaf-tips/).

To clear it:

1. **Remove the crust first.** Oregon State advises taking off the crust before leaching, removing no more than an inch of soil.
2. **Leach the pot.** Water thoroughly, wait about five minutes, then water again and let the excess run out of the drainage holes. [University of Maryland Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/mineral-and-fertilizer-salt-deposits-indoor-plants) suggests at least three times the pot's volume of fresh water, drained completely.
3. **Empty the saucer** so the pot does not sit in the salty runoff.
4. **Review your feeding.** Follow the label rate and the advice in [how to fertilize houseplants](/guides/fertilize-houseplants/).

Both extension services suggest leaching every four to six months. In severe cases, repot into fresh mix and scrub or replace a crusted pot.

## When white mold signals a bigger problem

- **The mix is still wet days after watering, smells sour, or the plant wilts in wet soil.** Check the roots for [root rot](/problems/root-rot/).
- **Tiny dark flies hover around the pot.** [Fungus gnats](/problems/fungus-gnats/) breed in the same damp surface layer.
- **The mold is back within days of scraping.** Watering, drainage or light is still off.
- **The crust and brown tips return quickly.** Look at your fertilizer rate and water source.
