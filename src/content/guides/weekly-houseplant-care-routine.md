A routine prevents neglect, but a rigid watering schedule creates its own problems. Pick one day to check every plant in the same order. Only the plants that pass the moisture check get watered.

## 1. Look before you touch

Stand back and compare each plant with the previous week. Look for:

- New leaves that are smaller, paler or farther apart
- A plant leaning strongly toward the window
- Sudden leaf drop, yellowing or wilting
- A pot that remains wet much longer than usual
- New spots, damaged edges or distorted growth

The direction of change matters more than one old blemish. A single scarred leaf is history; symptoms appearing across new growth suggest an active problem.

## 2. Check the potting mix

Feel below the surface. For many leafy houseplants, the top few centimetres can dry before watering; succulents, snake plants and ZZ plants usually need the mix to dry much farther. Use the plant's species guide rather than one universal rule.

Lift smaller pots as well. A dry pot is noticeably lighter than a freshly watered one, and learning that difference makes checks faster.

If the plant is ready, water slowly until the root ball is evenly wet and water leaves the drainage hole. Empty the saucer. If it is still damp, leave it alone and check again later.

## 3. Inspect for pests

Look at the newest growth, stem joints and leaf undersides. Watch for:

- Fine webbing or pale speckles
- Cottony clusters
- Brown or translucent bumps
- Sticky leaves or surfaces below the plant
- Tiny flies lifting from the soil

Small populations are easier to control. Isolate a suspicious plant before moving on, then identify the pest. See the guides for [spider mites](/problems/spider-mites/) and [fungus gnats](/problems/fungus-gnats/) if those signs match.

## 4. Remove only what is finished

Pick up fallen leaves and cut fully dead leaves or stems with clean scissors. Do not remove a partly green leaf merely because it is imperfect; the plant can still use it.

Wipe obvious dust from broad leaves when needed. A full cleaning does not have to happen every week—use the [leaf-cleaning guide](/guides/clean-houseplant-leaves/) when buildup becomes visible.

## 5. Check the position

Make sure a heater, air conditioner or seasonal sunbeam has not changed the plant's environment. A spot that worked in spring may become hot in summer or very dim in winter.

Rotate plants a quarter-turn occasionally if they are growing unevenly toward the window. Do not use rotation to compensate for an unsuitable location; measure and improve the light instead.

## Monthly and seasonal jobs

Keep the weekly routine short. Put slower tasks on a separate monthly or seasonal list:

- Clean leaves thoroughly
- Flush accumulated fertilizer salts from the potting mix
- Check whether roots are crowding drainage holes
- Adjust watering as day length and temperature change
- Fertilize only during active growth

Ten attentive minutes are more useful than a long rescue session after a problem has spread. Check consistently, change one thing at a time, and let the plant's response guide the next decision.
