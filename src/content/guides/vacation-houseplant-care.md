The safest travel plan starts with the plant's normal needs. Extra water cannot be stored safely in an already wet pot, and an untested watering gadget can fail in either direction.

## A few days before leaving

Check every plant rather than treating the collection as one unit. Group them into three practical categories:

1. **Fast-drying plants** in small pots, strong light or warm rooms
2. **Average leafy plants** that normally dry over a week or more
3. **Dry-tolerant plants** such as succulents, snake plants and ZZ plants

Watch how quickly each pot is drying. This gives you time to arrange help for the genuinely thirsty group without watering the others early.

Remove fallen leaves, check for pests and pause fertilizer. Feeding just before a trip encourages growth when nobody is there to observe water use or stress.

## Water by need, not by departure time

On the day before leaving, feel below the soil surface and lift smaller pots. Water only the plants that are ready.

When needed, water thoroughly until the root ball is evenly wet and runoff leaves the drainage hole. Let the pot drain completely and empty every saucer or cachepot. Never leave roots standing in water as a reserve.

If a plant is still damp, do not top it up. It will use the water already in the pot.

## Reduce avoidable stress

Move fast-drying plants out of hot direct sun for the trip, but keep them in useful indirect light. Do not put sun-loving plants into a dark room; the goal is to reduce extreme heat, not remove light.

Keep plants away from heating and air-conditioning vents. Group compatible plants loosely so the air around them changes less quickly, while leaving enough space for airflow.

Close sheer curtains if they normally protect foliage from intense afternoon sun. Avoid changing the thermostat to a temperature that would stress the plants.

## Use watering systems only after a test

A wick, reservoir or self-watering insert can work, but flow depends on the potting mix, container and height of the water source. Test the complete setup for several days while you are home.

Check that:

- The wick stays in contact with both water and potting mix
- The pot becomes evenly moist rather than saturated
- The reservoir lasts long enough
- Water cannot siphon onto furniture or floors

Do not introduce a new system on departure day.

## If someone is helping

Clear instructions prevent generous overwatering. Group only the plants likely to need attention and label them.

Write a short note such as: “Check this pot on Thursday. Water only if the top 5 cm is dry. If dry, pour one full jug slowly, let it drain, and empty the saucer.”

Do not ask a helper to diagnose, fertilize, repot or follow a different schedule for every plant unless they already know the collection.

## When you return

Inspect before watering. A wilted plant with dry soil may recover after a thorough soak. A wilted plant in wet soil needs diagnosis, not more water.

Return temporarily moved plants to their usual positions, but avoid stacking several care changes into one day. Resume fertilizer only after normal growth and watering have settled.

For most established houseplants, a calm plan beats an elaborate one: water the plants that need it, limit avoidable heat and test any substitute care before you go.
