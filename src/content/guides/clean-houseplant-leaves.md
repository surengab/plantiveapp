Clean leaves look better, make pests easier to see and allow you to inspect the plant closely. The safest cleaning method depends on the leaf surface. Plain lukewarm water is enough for routine dust.

## Before you start

Move the plant away from direct sun and check that the room is warm enough for wet foliage to dry. Support delicate stems with one hand instead of pulling leaves toward you.

Gather only what you need:

- A bowl of lukewarm water
- A soft, clean cloth
- A soft artist's brush for fuzzy leaves
- Clean scissors for fully dead material

Skip commercial leaf-shine products and household oils. They create residue and do not improve plant health.

## Broad, smooth leaves

For monstera, rubber plant, peace lily and similar foliage:

1. Dampen a soft cloth with lukewarm water and wring it out.
2. Support the underside of one leaf with your hand.
3. Wipe gently from the base toward the tip.
4. Use a clean part of the cloth for the next leaf.
5. Wipe the underside if it is dusty, checking for pests as you go.

Do not scrub spots that are part of the leaf tissue. A mineral mark may lift; damage caused by sun, cold or pests will not.

## Many small leaves

Small or sturdy plants can be rinsed in a sink or shower with a gentle stream of lukewarm water. Cover loose potting mix with your hand or a reusable cover so it does not wash out.

Tilt the pot and rinse both sides of the foliage. Let it drain completely before returning it to its saucer. Keep the plant out of harsh direct sun until the leaves are dry.

If the plant's crown is prone to rot, avoid leaving water trapped where leaves meet the stem. Use a cloth instead or blot the crown dry.

## Fuzzy or textured leaves

African violet and other fuzzy leaves hold water and show marks easily. Keep them dry. Use a soft brush to loosen dust, working with the direction of the hairs.

For deeply textured leaves, a soft brush can reach grooves that a cloth misses. Use almost no pressure.

## What cleaning reveals

Treat cleaning as an inspection. Look for fine webbing, pale stippling, sticky residue, cottony clusters, brown bumps or distorted new growth. Check the stem joints and undersides before putting the plant back.

If you find anything suspicious, isolate the plant. Match the visible signs to the [plant problem guides](/problems/) before choosing a treatment.

## Common mistakes

- **Using very cold or hot water.** Lukewarm water avoids temperature shock.
- **Cleaning in direct sun.** Wet leaves can mark or scorch in intense light.
- **Scrubbing hard.** Leaf tissue does not heal like skin.
- **Leaving the pot in runoff.** Let it drain and empty the saucer.
- **Cleaning every week without need.** Inspect weekly; wash only when dust has accumulated.

A clean plant is easier to read. You will notice pests, mineral buildup and new damage sooner, which is the real value of the job.
